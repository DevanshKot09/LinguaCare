## 2. Key concepts to encode in the app
- **DLD**: persistent language difficulty without a known biomedical cause (not explained by hearing loss, autism, intellectual disability, brain injury). Affects understanding and/or expression; persists across childhood and often into adulthood. Distinct from a *temporary late-talker delay*.
- **Domains affected**: receptive language, expressive language, vocabulary (word learning/finding), grammar/sentence formation, narrative, conversation/pragmatics, following multi-step instructions.
- **Presentation varies** by age and person; some children **compensate well** and are missed by screening.
- **Secondary impacts**: friendships, confidence/self-esteem, classroom participation, reading comprehension, figurative language (jokes/idioms), emotional expression, daily tasks.
- **Assessment** is done by qualified professionals (SLP/speech-language therapist; plus audiologist for hearing, psychologist as needed). Must consider hearing status, multilingual development, and rule out other explanations.
- **Causes**: multifactorial; strong family/genetic contribution studied; avoid blaming caregivers; some popular claims lack evidence (myth-buster content).
- **Support**: speech-language therapy (personalised goals, vocabulary, explicit grammar, narrative, conversation), classroom accommodations (visual schedules, simplified instructions without lowering expectations, pre-taught vocabulary, written + spoken instructions, fair assessment), caregiver routines (shared reading, expansions, no "conversation as test").
- **Tech principles**: screen without diagnosing, adaptive difficulty, honest accuracy claims, speech recognition limits for atypical speech, offline mode, accessible design, complement (not replace) therapy, strong child-data/voice-data protection.

## 3. Product guardrails derived from the content
1. Every screening result ends with *"This is not a diagnosis. Consider speaking with a speech-language professional."*
2. Non-blaming, non-stigmatising language everywhere ("person with DLD", strengths-first).
3. Practice must be low-pressure; never turn conversation into a test.
4. Clinical content must cite sources and be SLP-reviewed.
5. Progress claims use ranges/trends, not definitive "scores".
